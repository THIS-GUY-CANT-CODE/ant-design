01 · Story

Same family. Same street. Since 1938.

Rose opened on Bethnal Green Road in 1938 selling car and cycle parts. It's still owned by the same family, and it's now the shop people trust with the awkward key, the dead garage remote and the Saturday paint job. The brand should feel like the shop does: solid, helpful and a bit old-school, in the best way.

Positioning

The locksmith and hardware shop Bethnal Green has trusted for 88 years, with 684 five-star reviews to prove it.

02 · Logo

The Shop Key.

A classic shop key: a solid round bow and two brass teeth. It works like the old enamel shop sign, instantly readable from across Bethnal Green Road. The wordmark is just "Rose" in Bitter Black. Everyone round here already calls it that.

03 · Colour

Enamel sign, brass key.

Navy ink and cream paper come from old enamel shop signs. Rose red is the name and the emergency line, and brass is the keys. Keep rose red for urgent actions (emergency and call) so it always means "now".

04 · Type

Slab serif, like a shop sign.

Bitter (a slab serif) for headlines has the confidence of hand-painted shopfront lettering. Work Sans is for everything practical: prices, hours and services. Both are free on Google Fonts.

05 · Voice

Like the bloke behind the counter.

Short, practical and reassuring. Say what we can do, what it costs and how long it takes. No scare tactics about burglars.

Do

"Worn key? Bring it in. We'll probably cut it while you wait."

Don't

"Don't risk your family's safety with a sub-par key!"

Do

"Locked out? Call 07944 199472."

Don't

"Our state-of-the-art rapid response solutions..."

Do

"Same family since 1938."

Don't

"London's #1 best locksmith guaranteed."

06 · Imagery

The shop, the hands, the keys.

Real photos of the shopfront, the key-cutting machine mid-cut, walls of blanks, paint being mixed and the family behind the counter. Warm daylight and close-ups of brass and steel. No stock photos of a burglar in a balaclava.
