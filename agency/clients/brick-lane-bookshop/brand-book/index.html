01 · Story

From a market stall to Brick Lane.

It started in April 1977 as the THAP Bookshop in Watney Market, part of the Tower Hamlets Arts Project. It moved to Whitechapel in 1980, became Eastside in 1994, and is now Brick Lane Bookshop. For nearly fifty years it has hosted readings by writers from Andrea Levy to Iain Sinclair. The brand should feel like the street: bold, creative and proudly independent, but still a bookshop you want to linger in.

02 · Logo

One brick is a book.

A wall of bricks where one brick is a different colour, like a book on a shelf. It's Brick Lane, it's bookshelves, and it's the one independent that stands out on the street. The wordmark is set in Space Grotesk, with body text in Newsreader for a bookish read.

03 · Colour

Brick, ink, paper.

Brick red is the street, ink black is print, and paper cream is the page. It's a deliberately graphic, poster-like palette, to set Brick Lane Bookshop apart from softer "cosy bookshop" brands.

04 · Type

Street grotesk, reading serif.

Space Grotesk has the edge of street posters and zines. Newsreader is a serif made for reading, so body copy feels like a book. Both are free on Google Fonts.

05 · Voice

Sharp, local, generous.

Talk like a bookseller who knows the street's history and every good new novel. Confident and a bit witty, never pretentious.
