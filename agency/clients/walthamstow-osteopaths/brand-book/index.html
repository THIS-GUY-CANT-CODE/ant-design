01 · Story

A restored shop with a long memory.

Iain Chapman and Stephen Moore founded the practice in 2000 and in 2002 took on No.72 St Mary Road, a derelict Victorian shop that had been a brewery, a music shop and a hairdresser. They restored the shopfront and the original quarry tiles. The brand should feel like the building: calm, crafted, local and unhurried.

Positioning

Walthamstow Village's long-standing osteopathy practice, run by the same two founders in a building locals already know.

02 · Logo

The Arch & Spine.

The arched Victorian doorway of No.72, with a column of four dots down the middle: a spine, and the steps of a treatment. It's calm and architectural, and it works as small as a favicon. The wordmark is set in Newsreader.

03 · Colour

Moss, limewash, quarry tile.

Deep moss green (a Victorian shopfront colour) is the anchor, with warm limewash walls behind it. Terracotta comes from the original quarry tiles and is kept for booking actions and small details.

04 · Type

A gentle serif and a friendly sans.

Newsreader brings a quiet, bookish warmth, with italics for emphasis. Manrope is clear and modern for everything practical. Keep headings light (400 weight), because health brands should never shout.

05 · Voice

Calm, clear, never clinical.

Explain things as you would in the treatment room. Describe what we do, not what we cure: osteopathy advertising has to follow ASA/CAP rules, so no promises of outcomes.

Do

"Never seen an osteopath? Here's what happens."

Don't

"Our proprietary musculoskeletal protocol…"

Do

"We'll explain what we think is going on and agree a plan together."

Don't

"Guaranteed to fix your back pain in 3 sessions!"

Do

"Hands-on care at No.72."

Don't

"Holistic wellness journey for mind, body and soul."

06 · Imagery

Light, hands, the building.

Soft daylight through the shopfront windows, the quarry tiles, hands at work (always with consent and dignity), and the founders in the rooms. Avoid skeleton models and the blue-gloved clinical stock look.
