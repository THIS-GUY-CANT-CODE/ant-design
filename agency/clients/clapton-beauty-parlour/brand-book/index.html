01 · Story

A 1930 salon with a famous guest.

Jean and Emanuel Manning opened the Clapton Beauty Parlour in 1930. In the 1940s a teenage Vidal Sassoon worked from it while his own salon was repaired after bomb damage, and it was later the "in shop" for the Beverley Sisters. The brand leans into that glamour, with Art Deco elegance, but stays warm and neighbourly. It's still a local salon on Lower Clapton Road.

02 · Logo

The Sunrise Fan.

An Art Deco sunburst, the defining motif of 1930, the year the Parlour opened. It also reads as a salon hand mirror or an open fan. Gold rays sit on lacquer black with a blush-pink sun. The wordmark is set in Bodoni Moda, the high-contrast type of a 1930s fashion plate.

03 · Colour

Lacquer, blush and gilt.

Black lacquer and gold come from 1930s salon mirrors and deco shopfronts. Blush pink is the powder-room warmth, and mint is a quiet deco accent for small details. Use gold for lines and ornament, never for body text.

04 · Type

Fashion-plate serif, deco sans.

Bodoni Moda has the high contrast of 1930s beauty adverts, and Jost is a geometric sans in the spirit of Futura (1927). Set labels in wide-tracked caps for the deco signage feel. Both are free on Google Fonts.

05 · Voice

Glamorous, never snooty.

A touch of old-Hollywood charm in headlines, and friendly, plain English everywhere else. Tell the history with pride, but never make a regular feel they're not fancy enough.

Do

"Pull up a chair."

Don't

"Exclusive luxury hair experiences for the discerning client."

Do

"The day Vidal Sassoon borrowed the keys."

Don't

"Vidal Sassoon trained here!" (He didn't. Keep the story accurate.)

Do

"Doing Clapton's hair since 1930."

Don't

"Best salon in London!!!"

06 · Imagery

Mirrors, hands and archive.

Shoot finished looks in the salon mirrors with warm tungsten light, close-ups of hands at work, and the family's archive photos (scanned, never filtered). Avoid generic stock models and pink-neon Instagram clichés.
