01 · Story

Ninety years of Hackney flowers.

Kelly's has been a family-run florist in Hackney for more than ninety years, arranging bouquets for births, birthdays and weddings, and tributes for goodbyes. The brand must hold both joy and grief, so it's warm, gentle and never twee.

Positioning

The real family florist on Well Street, not a relay website. Arranged by people who know Hackney.

02 · Logo

The Single Tulip.

One hand-drawn tulip: simple, generous and unmistakably a florist. It works on a ribbon, a card tag or a van. The wordmark is just "Kelly's" in Cormorant Garamond, the name Hackney already uses.

03 · Colour

Aubergine, peony, stem.

Deep aubergine gives the brand dignity, which matters for tributes. Peony pink brings the joy, and stem green grounds it in nature. Cream keeps everything soft. Never use bright red or harsh black: flowers should always be the loudest thing on the page.

04 · Type

Elegant serif, gentle sans.

Cormorant Garamond is graceful for headlines and card messages. Nunito Sans is soft and legible for prices and ordering. Both are free on Google Fonts.

05 · Voice

Warm, gentle, personal.

Talk like the florist behind the counter who has helped thousands of people find the right flowers. Be joyful for celebrations, quiet and kind for tributes, and never salesy about grief.

Do

"When you're saying goodbye, we're here to help."

Don't

"Funeral flowers from just £29.99! Order now!"

Do

"Tell us the occasion and your budget, and we'll suggest something lovely."

Don't

"Browse our 500+ SKU catalogue."

Do

"Hackney's family florist for over 90 years."

Don't

"Best flower services in London."

06 · Imagery

Real stems, real hands.

Photograph actual arrangements from the shop in soft daylight against kraft paper or the shop wall, hands tying a bouquet, and the Well Street shopfront with buckets outside. Tribute photos should be respectful and simple, never staged with props.
