01 · Story

Saved by locals, for locals.

The Queen's Head has stood on Flamborough Street since 1827. It's Grade II listed, believed to have inspired the Queen Vic, and the Queen Mother once famously pulled a pint here. In December 2023 a group of locals reopened it as a free house at community prices. The brand should feel like the best version of a proper East End boozer: gilded, warm, a bit cheeky and completely unpretentious.

02 · Logo

The Crown & Pint.

A crown sitting on a full pint: the Queen's Head, and the Queen Mother's famous pint, in one mark. It's gold on oxblood like a Victorian gilded pub sign, and it works on a beer mat, a chalkboard or a hanging sign. The wordmark is set in Abril Fatface, the heavy display type of old signwriting.

03 · Colour

Oxblood, gold leaf, bar tile.

Oxblood and gold leaf come straight from Victorian pub signs and etched mirrors. Bottle-green tile is the classic East End pub frontage. Cream is the nicotine-free ceiling we all remember as nicotine yellow.

04 · Type

Signwriter's display, honest sans.

Abril Fatface has the fat, high-contrast look of hand-painted pub signs and chalkboards. Karla is plain and friendly for the menu, prices and quiz dates. Both are free on Google Fonts.

05 · Voice

Like the landlord at the bar.

Warm, funny and to the point. Be proud of the history without being precious about it. Always make it clear everyone is welcome. Never trade on the royal family in a way that implies endorsement.

Do

"Pull up a stool."

Don't

"An elevated gastropub experience."

Do

"The Queen Mother pulled a pint here. You can too, if you're buying."

Don't

"By Royal Appointment." (We're not, and it's a protected phrase.)

Do

"Limehouse's local, run by locals."

Don't

"London's best pub, guaranteed."

06 · Imagery

Pints, faces, the bar.

Warm, grainy photos of regulars (with permission), the bar and the tiles, quiz night mid-laugh, and the pub from the street at dusk. Archive photos of the Queen Mother's visit if the rights can be cleared. No stock beer splashes.
