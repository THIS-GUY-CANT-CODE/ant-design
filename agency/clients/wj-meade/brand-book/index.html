01 · Story

Seventy years. Three owners. One name.

Walter Joseph Meade opened on Hoe Street, Walthamstow, in 1953. Seven decades and only three owners later, W J Meade has five offices across East and North London and is still independent. The brand should feel established without feeling old: London stock brick, a navy front door and a firm handshake.

02 · Logo

The Roofline M.

An M drawn as two East London gables, with a brick-red front door. It's the Meade initial and a terraced house in one mark. It sits in a navy tile that works as a sign, an app icon or a board topper. The wordmark is set in DM Serif Display.

03 · Colour

Navy door, stock brick.

Navy is the classic front-door colour. London stock brick yellow (the colour of every Victorian terrace in E3) is the warm accent, and brick red is kept for the one thing that matters most: the valuation button.

05 · Voice

Straight-talking and local.

Talk like the negotiator who's been on these streets for years: specific, honest and no agent-speak. Property descriptions must be accurate (Consumer Protection Regulations), so no puffery.

Do

"Two minutes from Roman Road Market, with a south-facing garden."

Don't

"Stunning, must-see, deceptively spacious!"

Do

"We'll tell you what it's really worth."

Don't

"Guaranteed highest valuation in E3!"

Do

"Since 1953."

Don't

"Your premier property solutions partner."

06 · Imagery

Streets, doors, daylight.

Straight-on shots of East London terraces and front doors, bright interiors shot level, and the team out on the streets they cover. Avoid ultra-wide lenses that make rooms look bigger than they are. Buyers notice, and it costs trust.
