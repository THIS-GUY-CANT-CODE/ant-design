What it is now

A fast, single-file site with no framework, built for phones first, with a signature moment only this business could own.

01

The key wall

Rows of key blanks on hooks that swing, with real spring physics, when you brush your cursor or finger along them.

02

A working lock, explained

An animated pin-tumbler cross-section. The right key slides in, every pin lifts to the shear line, and it turns green. Try the wrong key and it stays red.

03

Two front doors

A red emergency route with the emergency number, and a brass "pop in" route for keys, remotes and paint.

04

Your name, cut as a key

Type any name and it's turned into a unique key profile, with a cutting wheel running along the blade.

05

A Dulux wall mixer

Tap a paint chip and a whole room repaints itself, while the tin gives a shake.

06

Open or closed, at a glance

A live status pill, today's hours highlighted, and an OPEN or CLOSED sign in the illustrated shop window.

03

The brand pitch

Same family. Same street. Since 1938.

Heritage without the dust. Navy and brass come straight from the shopfront and the keys on the wall, and rose red is kept for the emergency line only, so it always means urgent. Bitter is a sturdy slab serif that looks as good painted on a fascia as it does on a phone.

  • A key in a rose roundel: the name and the trade in one mark
  • Red means emergency, only ever
  • Voice: straight-talking and helpful. "If it opens, locks or needs fixing, start here."
