# Cold Outreach 30 — Email Templates That Don't Sound Like Cold Email

30 copy-paste openers, follow-ups and closers for freelancers who hate selling.
Rules baked in: no "I hope this finds you well", no fake flattery, every email under 120
words, one clear ask. Replace [brackets]; delete any line that isn't true for you.

---

## A · First-Touch Openers (10)

**1. The specific compliment + gap**
> Subject: [Their site] — one thing
> Noticed [genuinely specific thing you liked] on [their site/product]. One thing that might
> be costing you: [specific gap you can fix]. I do [service] for [niche] — recent example:
> [one-line result]. Worth a 15-minute look this week?

**2. The mutual point of reference**
> Subject: via [person/community]
> I'm in [community] too — saw your post about [topic]. That problem usually comes down to
> [cause]. I've fixed it for [similar business]; happy to share exactly how, no strings.

**3. The mini-audit**
> Subject: 3 quick wins on [their site]
> I spent 10 minutes on [their site] and found three things worth fixing: 1) [specific],
> 2) [specific], 3) [specific]. Want the details? I'll send them over either way — if you'd
> rather have someone just do it, that's what I do.

**4. The competitor angle (respectful)**
> Subject: what [competitor type] are doing differently
> Businesses like yours in [niche] have started [tactic] — it's moving [metric]. You're
> well-placed to do it better because [genuine reason]. I help [niche] with exactly this.
> 15 minutes?

**5. The "I made you something"**
> Subject: made this for you
> Rather than describe what I do, I [mocked up / drafted / sketched] [small deliverable] for
> [their business] — attached. Yours either way. If you'd like the full version, let's talk.

**6. The timely trigger**
> Subject: congrats on [event]
> Saw [their news — launch, funding, hire, move]. That usually means [need] lands on
> someone's plate soon. That's my lane: [one-liner + proof]. Want me to take it off yours?

**7. The straight shooter**
> Subject: [service] for [company]
> No tricks: I'm a [role] taking on [N] new clients this quarter. I work with [niche] on
> [outcome] — e.g. [result]. If that's a current headache, reply and I'll send three ideas
> specific to you before we ever talk money.

**8. The question-only**
> Subject: quick question
> Who handles [function] at [company]? I've got a suggestion about [specific thing] that's
> probably worth 2 minutes of their time.

**9. The result-first**
> Subject: [X result] for [similar business]
> Last month I [specific result with number] for a [their type of business]. The method
> would map onto [company] almost 1:1. Open to hearing how?

**10. The honest beginner (use it — it works)**
> Subject: one free [deliverable], one condition
> I'm building my portfolio in [niche] and I'm good at [skill]. I'll do [small deliverable]
> for free — my only condition is a testimonial if (and only if) you love it. I'm offering
> this to three businesses; you're on the list because [genuine reason].

## B · Follow-Ups (8)

**11. Day 3 — the nudge:** > Floating this back up — the [original offer] stands. Yes/no is genuinely fine.
**12. Day 7 — add value:** > Since I wrote, [new idea/article/example relevant to them]. Made me think of your [thing] again. Still happy to walk you through the original idea.
**13. Day 14 — the alternative:** > Maybe [original ask] is the wrong shape. Would [smaller version] be more useful? Half the size, same direction.
**14. The wrong-person pivot:** > If this isn't your desk, who should I ask? One name and I'll leave you alone.
**15. The proof drop:** > Just wrapped [project] — [one-line result]. Same approach I suggested for you. Door's open.
**16. The deadline (true ones only):** > Heads-up: my [month] slots close on [date]. If the timing's off, no problem — say "later" and I'll come back in [quarter].
**17. The graceful last word:** > Last note from me — I'll assume the timing isn't right and stop nudging. If that changes, my earlier emails have everything. Good luck with [their project].
**18. The resurrection (90 days later):** > You crossed my mind — [new relevant observation about their business]. The offer from [month] still stands, updated: [refreshed one-liner].

## C · Replies That Close (7)

**19. "What do you charge?"** > Depends on scope, so I'll give you the honest range: [X–Y] for something like this. If that's in-budget, a 15-minute call gets you an exact number. If it's not, no hard feelings — tell me and I'll point you somewhere cheaper.
**20. "Can you send more info?"** > Happy to — but a generic PDF won't help you. Give me [one detail] and I'll send a plan specific to [company] instead. Better use of your inbox.
**21. "We already have someone."** > That's good news, not bad. Keep them — a second pair of hands for [overflow/specialty] is how most of my clients start. When something doesn't fit their plate, that's my email.
**22. "Not right now."** > Understood. When does the timing loosen up? I'll come back then and not before.
**23. "Too expensive."** > Fair. Two options: [smaller scope] at [lower price], or keep my number for when the budget matches the ambition. Both fine.
**24. The call booking:** > Great — here's my calendar: [link]. Pick anything. So the call is useful, I'll bring [prepared thing]; could you have [one input] to hand?
**25. The proposal cover note:** > Proposal attached — 2 pages, no padding. The number is on page 2, the reasoning on page 1. Read it in that order. Questions welcome; silence after [date] and I'll check in once.

## D · Housekeeping (5)

**26. The referral ask (existing contact):** > Working with you on [project] was a highlight this year. Who's one person in your world with a similar headache? An intro line from you is worth ten cold emails from me.
**27. The testimonial ask:** > Could you reply with 2 sentences: what was the problem, and what changed? I'll format it — you approve the final wording.
**28. The re-engage (past client):** > It's been [time] since [project] — how's it holding up? Two things I could improve now that didn't exist then: [idea 1], [idea 2]. Want either?
**29. The price-rise notice:** > From [date], new projects move to [new rate]. Existing clients (you) keep [current rate] until [later date]. Booking anything before then locks it in.
**30. The "I'm full" (send it proudly):** > Thanks for asking — I'm booked until [date]. I can: 1) waitlist you for then, 2) recommend [name], or 3) both. Which works?

---

*Rules for using this pack: send 10 a day, personalised (the [brackets] are the work);
track replies in a sheet; follow up exactly 3 times, then stop. Volume without
personalisation is spam — and it doesn't work anyway.*
