# The ATS-Friendly CV Pack — 3 Templates + The Rules That Get Interviews

Most CVs are rejected by software before a human sees them. These three templates are built
to pass Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) AND read well to the recruiter who follows.
Copy the structure into a plain Word/Google Doc. No tables, no columns, no graphics — that's
not a style choice, it's how ATS parsing works.

---

## The 10 ATS rules (break none of them)

1. One column. Tables, text boxes and sidebars scramble the parse.
2. Standard headings, exactly: Professional Summary · Work Experience · Skills · Education.
3. File as .docx or PDF *only if the ad allows PDF* (when unsure: .docx).
4. Filename: `Firstname-Lastname-CV.docx` — it's visible in their system forever.
5. Mirror the job ad's exact keywords ("stakeholder management", not "handling stakeholders")
   — ATS matches strings, not synonyms.
6. Dates as `MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY`. Gaps are fine; unexplained formats aren't.
7. No photo, no age, no full address (city is enough), no "references available".
8. Bullets start with a verb, contain a number where humanly possible.
9. Two pages maximum; one if under ~5 years' experience.
10. One CV per application, tweaked to that ad's keywords. (15 minutes, using the checklist below.)

## Template 1 — The Standard (90% of applications)

```
FIRSTNAME LASTNAME
City · phone · email · linkedin.com/in/you

PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY
[Job title from the ad] with [X] years' experience in [their sector]. Known for
[strength #1 with a number] and [strength #2]. Currently [one-line status].

WORK EXPERIENCE

Job Title — Company, City                                    MMM YYYY – Present
• [Verb] [what] achieving [number/outcome]
• [Verb] [what], which [business result]
• [Verb] [what] across [scope — team size, budget, volume]

Job Title — Company, City                                    MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY
• (3–4 bullets, same pattern; older roles get fewer bullets)

SKILLS
[8–12 comma-separated skills — lifted from the job ad where true]

EDUCATION
Qualification — Institution, Year
```

## Template 2 — The Career Changer

Same skeleton as Template 1 with two swaps:
- **Summary states the bridge:** "Moving from [old field] into [new field], bringing
  [transferable skill] proven by [evidence]."
- **Add a section above Work Experience:**
```
RELEVANT PROJECTS & TRAINING
Project/Course — context                                     YYYY
• What you built/learned + a number that proves effort or outcome
```
Work Experience bullets then emphasise transferable actions (led, analysed, budgeted,
trained) over old-industry jargon.

## Template 3 — The Graduate / First Job

Order: Summary → Education (with modules/grade if strong) → Projects → Any Work → Skills.
The trick: treat projects like jobs.
```
PROJECTS
Dissertation / Group project / Personal build                YYYY
• What you did, tools used, and one measurable detail (sample size, users, grade, deadline)

WORK EXPERIENCE (any job counts)
Barista — Cafe, City                                        MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY
• Served 200+ customers daily while [reliability/teamwork evidence]
```
Nobody expects experience; they're screening for evidence you finish things.

## The 15-minute tailoring checklist (per application)

- [ ] Paste the job ad into a doc; highlight every repeated skill/requirement
- [ ] Get the top 5 highlighted phrases into your Summary and Skills — verbatim, where true
- [ ] Reorder your first job's bullets so the most ad-relevant is first
- [ ] Match your title line to theirs if it's honest (e.g. "Marketing Executive" vs "Marketing Officer")
- [ ] Re-read bullet one — would a stranger know what you achieved? Add the number.
- [ ] Save as new file: `Firstname-Lastname-CV-[Company].docx`
