# The ADHD-Friendly Daily Planner

A printable one-page daily system designed around how ADHD brains actually work — not
against them. No 6am routines, no 30-slot schedules, no shame. Print a stack, keep them
by the kettle.

Built on four principles: **shrink the day** (3 things, not 30) · **externalise everything**
(the page is your working memory) · **anchor to events, not clock times** · **make starting
tiny** (the 2-minute ignition).

---

## THE DAILY PAGE (print this section, one per day)

```

──────────────────────────── THE ONLY 3 ────────────────────────────
The 3 things that make today a win. Not 10. Three.

  1. ______________________________________  ⚡ first 2-minute step: ______________
  2. ______________________________________  ⚡ first 2-minute step: ______________
  3. ______________________________________  ⚡ first 2-minute step: ______________

  ⭐ If I only do ONE, it's #____  (decide NOW, not at 4pm)

──────────────────────────── ANCHORS ────────────────────────────
Tie tasks to events, not times. "After X, I do Y."

  After waking up ............ ______________________________

  After dinner ............... ______________________________

──────────────────────────── BRAIN DUMP ────────────────────────────
Every intrusive "oh I must also—" goes HERE, not into your day.

  □ ________________________   □ ________________________
  □ ________________________   □ ________________________
  □ ________________________   □ ________________________
  (These are NOT today's jobs. They're captured. That's enough.)

  Meds taken:  AM □   PM □        Water: □ □ □ □ □ □
  Ate actual food: □ morning  □ midday  □ evening
  Moved my body (any amount counts): □

──────────────────────────── DOPAMINE MENU ────────────────────────────
Pre-planned rewards — pick BEFORE you start, claim AFTER a block:
  Quick (2 min): ____________________  Medium (15 min): ____________________
  Big (tonight): ____________________

──────────────────────────── SHUTDOWN (2 minutes, evening) ────────────────────────────
  □ Tomorrow's ⭐ ONE thing written on tomorrow's page: ______________________
  □ Tomorrow's first 2-minute step decided
  □ Keys/wallet/bag where they live
  □ Say it out loud: "Done is done. The list is not me."
```

## HOW TO USE IT (the honest manual)

**The Only 3.** ADHD planning fails at the *selection* step — everything feels equally
urgent. Force-rank to three the night before, when tomorrow is hypothetical and choosing is
cheap. The ⚡ 2-minute step matters more than the task: "email Sarah" starts with "open a
reply and type one sentence." You're not committing to the task, only to the ignition.

**Anchors beat clocks.** Time-blindness makes "2:30pm" invisible until 2:47pm. Events are
felt: lunch happens, logging off happens. Chain the habit to the event.

**The Brain Dump is load-bearing.** Mid-task thoughts ("I should also renew the car
insurance!") hijack the day because your brain doesn't trust you to remember them. Write it
in the dump and your brain lets go. Review the dumps once a week (see below) — most items
die of natural causes, which is them telling you they never mattered.

**Dopamine menu.** Decide rewards in advance because in-the-moment "breaks" become 90
minutes of scrolling. A named, pre-chosen reward closes the loop honestly.

**Energy circle.** On a 🪫 day, cross out items 2 and 3 without guilt. The system bends so
you don't break the system.

## THE WEEKLY RESET (10 minutes, same trigger every week — e.g. Sunday after dinner)

1. Gather the week's pages. Read the Brain Dumps only.
2. Anything that appears twice+ becomes a candidate for next week's Only 3s.
3. Cross out everything else. (Yes, all of it. If it matters it will come back.)
4. Fill in tomorrow's page: 3 things, one ⭐, ignition steps.
5. Put the pen back where the pages live. The system dies if the pen wanders.

## STARTER IDEAS FOR IGNITION STEPS

Open the document and write one bad sentence · Put trainers on (not "go for a run") ·
Fill the sink (not "do the dishes") · Open the email and type "Hi" · Put ONE item back
where it lives · Set a 10-minute timer and permit yourself to stop when it rings (you
usually won't — but the permission is what gets you in).
