**Description:**

"Do I need to tell HMRC about my Vinted sales?"

If you've ever googled that at 11pm, this spreadsheet is for you.

Since the platforms started reporting seller data to HMRC, every casual seller has the same
three worries. This tracker answers all of them automatically:

- **Am I over the £1,000 trading allowance?** — A live yes/no indicator that tells you exactly
  when you need to register for Self Assessment (and when you can relax).
- **What's my REAL profit?** — Log each sale with its platform fees and postage; net profit
  per item and per month is automatic.
- **Which deduction method saves me more tax?** — HMRC lets you choose between the £1,000
  trading allowance and your actual expenses. The tracker computes both and tells you which
  one leaves more money in your pocket.

**What's inside:**
- 📊 Dashboard — profit, HMRC registration indicator, best-method picker, monthly breakdown
- 🏷️ Sales Log — 500 rows with platform dropdowns (Vinted, eBay, Etsy, Depop, FB, Amazon)
- 📦 Cost Log — stock hauls, packaging, postage supplies
- ⚙️ Tax Settings — allowance and rates all editable
- 📖 Worked examples + 2-minute guide

Works in **Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice**. No macros, no subscription.

*Note: estimates for individuals; selling your own second-hand belongings generally isn't
taxable trading. A planning tool, not tax advice.*

>
> Seeing loads of confusion since the HMRC reporting rules kicked in. Quick facts:
> 1) Selling your own old clothes = generally not taxable, full stop, any amount.
> 2) If you BUY (or make) to sell: you get a £1,000 trading allowance per tax year — measured
>    on gross sales income, not profit. £1,001 of gross sales with £900 of costs still means

> 3) Once over it, you choose: deduct the flat £1,000 allowance OR your actual costs —
>    whichever is bigger. Low-cost flippers almost always win with the allowance.
